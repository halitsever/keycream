# Cherry MX Blue: the clicky switch, explained

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

The Cherry MX Blue is the switch most people picture when they think of a loud mechanical keyboard. It has a bump you can feel and a click you can hear, both at the moment the key registers. This post covers how Cherry builds that click, the numbers on Cherry's own spec sheet, and a recording of an MX Blue keyboard you can play here.

## Where the click comes from

Cherry's product page explains it directly: the switching slide (the stem) of the MX Blue is made of two parts. When you press the key and the white part hits the blue stem after the tactile point, it makes the click. So the click is a separate event from the key bottoming out, and you hear it even if you stop pressing halfway.

Cherry calls the switch "tactile & clicky". In its current lineup only two MX switches are listed "with click": MX Blue and MX Green, which Cherry describes as "loud, robust and noticeable".

## Cherry's specs

From Cherry's MX2A Blue product page, checked on 28 November 2026:

| | Cherry MX Blue |
| --- | --- |
| Type | tactile and clicky |
| Operating force | 60 cN in the page highlights |
| Actuation force | 50 cN in the properties table |
| Initial force | 25 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.2 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm |
| Rated life | over 50 million keystrokes |

Like the Brown page, the Blue page lists two force figures without explaining them. Note the longer pre-travel: 2.2 mm against 2.0 mm on MX Red and Brown. KeyCream's pack doesn't say which generation of MX Blue was recorded, so these numbers describe the switch, not the recording.

## How it compares

| | MX Red | MX Brown | MX Blue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | linear | tactile | tactile and clicky |
| Bump | no | yes | yes |
| Click | no | no | yes |

[Cherry MX Red vs Brown vs Blue](/blog/cherry-mx-red-vs-brown-vs-blue) puts all three side by side with players. For the click mechanism in general, including how to tell a clicky switch by sound alone, see [clicky switches](/blog/clicky-switches).

## Is it too loud?

That depends on where you type. The click is the design, so lube or foam won't remove it the way they soften a linear switch. In an office or on calls people often switch to a quieter type. If you want the sound but not the noise for everyone around you, an app that plays the recording through your headphones gives you one without the other.
