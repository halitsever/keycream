# Creamy keyboard sound: what makes switches sound creamy

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

"Creamy" is one of the words keyboard enthusiasts use for how a keyboard sounds. It is not a measurement and nobody standardized it, so this post sticks to what people generally mean by it, which parts of a keyboard change the sound, and a recording you can listen to instead of reading adjectives.

## What "creamy" usually means

Among keyboard hobbyists the sound words roughly line up like this:

| Word | What people usually mean |
| --- | --- |
| Creamy | smooth and soft, no sharp edge, no scratch |
| Thocky | deep, low and muffled |
| Clacky | bright, higher-pitched, sharp |
| Clicky | a distinct click from the switch itself, as in Cherry MX Blue |

These overlap and people disagree about the edges. A keyboard can be described as both creamy and thocky.

## What changes the sound

A key press makes noise in a few places: the stem rubbing against the housing on the way down, the stem hitting the bottom, the keycap, the plate and the case around them, and the spring and stem returning. Anything that changes one of those changes the sound.

**The switch.** Linear switches have no bump and no click, so the sound is mostly the bottom-out and the return. That makes them the common choice for a smooth sound. Tactile switches add a bump, and clicky ones add a click mechanism you can hear on purpose.

**Switch materials.** NovelKeys Cream switches have a housing and stem made of POM, a slippery plastic, which is where the "self-lubricating" description in product listings comes from.

**Lubrication.** Lubricant on the stem and spring reduces scratch and spring noise. Many switches come factory-lubed; others are lubed by hand.

**Keycaps.** Thicker keycaps and PBT plastic generally sound lower than thin ABS keycaps. The Cherry MX packs in KeyCream were recorded with PBT keycaps.

**Stabilizers.** The long keys (space, shift, return) have stabilizers that can rattle. A rattling space bar is the fastest way to lose a smooth sound.

**Case and foam.** A hollow case resonates. Foam between the plate and the case, or under the PCB, damps that.

## Making your keyboard sound more creamy

If you are changing a real keyboard, the usual order of cheap to expensive:

1. Fix stabilizer rattle.
2. Add foam to the case if it is hollow.
3. Try different keycaps, ideally thick PBT.
4. Lube or swap the switches, toward smooth linears.

Change one thing at a time and listen in between. The same switch sounds different in a different case.

## A creamy sound on a Mac keyboard

A laptop keyboard will not sound creamy, and no software changes how it feels. What software can change is what you hear. [KeyCream](/) is a free menu bar app for macOS 14 and later that plays one of seven real switch recordings for each key you press, NovelKeys Cream by default. It levels every key so none jumps out, and it only reads which key went down, never what you type. See [Privacy](/guide/privacy).
