# ASMR keyboard sounds on a Mac

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Keyboard ASMR is the sound of typing on a mechanical keyboard, enjoyed for its own sake: in long videos, in streams, or under your own fingers while you work. People reach it in three ways, and this post covers all three, with recordings you can play here. One of them is KeyCream, the free Mac app this site is about, so read that part with that in mind.

## What makes the sound

Most of what people like in keyboard ASMR comes from mechanical switches and the keyboard around them: the stem hitting the bottom, the key springing back, and the keycaps and case shaping that impact. Smooth linear switches give the softer, deeper end; clicky switches give a sharp, regular click. The words people use for it, thocky, creamy and clacky, are covered in [what is thock](/blog/thocky-keyboard).

## Way 1: watch or listen to recordings

Typing ASMR videos and audio are the easiest start, with nothing to install. The limit is that the sound is someone else's typing on someone else's keyboard, at their pace.

## Way 2: a mechanical keyboard

The real thing gives you the feel as well as the sound, and lets you tune it with switches, keycaps and foam. It costs money, takes desk space, and the people around you hear it too. If you go this way, [keyboard switch types](/blog/keyboard-switch-types) and [tactile vs linear switches](/blog/tactile-vs-linear-switches) help with the first choice.

## Way 3: real recordings, played as you type

A keyboard sound app plays a recording of a mechanical switch each time you press a key on the keyboard you already have. The feel stays the same; the sound becomes a mechanical keyboard's.

KeyCream does this on a Mac:

- **Seven packs recorded from real keyboards:** NovelKeys Cream, Cherry MX Black, Red, Brown and Blue, Everglide Crystal Purple and Everglide Oreo. Each pack is leveled when it loads, so switching doesn't make it louder or quieter.
- **Key down only.** A sound plays when a key goes down, including modifier keys. Releasing a key and the repeats macOS types while you hold one stay silent.
- **Free and open source,** for macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon and Intel.

To try the sounds before installing anything, type in the [switch sound board](/sounds). It plays the same recordings in your browser.

## Keeping it to yourself

The appeal of ASMR typing is personal; the noise for others isn't. With an app you control that:

- **Output Device** sends key sounds to your headphones while everything else plays elsewhere.
- **Lower During Calls and Media** drops key sounds to 5% while apps such as Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Slack or Spotify are open.
- **On/Off Shortcut** turns the sounds on or off from any app.

All three are described in [Using KeyCream](/guide/usage). The app needs the Input Monitoring permission to hear key presses, and [Privacy](/guide/privacy) explains exactly what it does with them: it gets which key went down as a number, never what you type, and stores nothing.

## Other apps

KeyCream isn't the only option. Mechvibes runs on Windows, macOS and Linux and can load custom packs, and Klack is a paid Mac app. [KeyCream vs Klack vs Mechvibes](/guide/keycream-vs-klack-vs-mechvibes) compares them.
