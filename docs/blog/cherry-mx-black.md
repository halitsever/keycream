# Cherry MX Black: Cherry's heavy linear switch

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

The Cherry MX Black is the heavier sibling of the MX Red: same straight, bump-free stroke, a stronger spring. It has been around for decades and still has a following among people who like a key that pushes back. This post covers what Cherry's spec sheet says about it, how it differs from Red, and a recording of it you can play here.

## Cherry's specs

From Cherry's MX2A Black product page, checked on 28 November 2026:

| | Cherry MX Black |
| --- | --- |
| Type | linear, no click |
| Operating force | 60 cN |
| Initial force | 30 cN |
| Final force | 110 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.0 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm |
| Rated life | over 100 million keystrokes |

The force figures describe the spring: about 30 cN to start moving the key, 60 cN where it actuates, and 110 cN at the bottom of the stroke. Cherry also lists factory-applied lubricant and a "noise-reducing barrel spring design" for the MX2A generation. KeyCream's pack doesn't say which generation was recorded, so the table describes the switch, not the recording.

## Black vs Red

| | MX Red | MX Black |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | linear | linear |
| Operating force | 45 cN | 60 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.0 mm | 2.0 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm |

Same shape, a third more force on the Black. A heavier spring means resting fingers are less likely to press a key by accident, and the key returns with more push. It also means more effort over a long day. Neither is better on paper. [Linear switches](/blog/linear-keyboard-switches) plays both recordings next to each other.

## What you hear

With no bump and no click, a linear switch's sound comes mostly from the stem hitting the bottom and springing back up, plus the keycap and the case around it. That is why the same switch can sound quite different in two keyboards, and why a recording tells you more than an adjective. If you want to know where this sound sits next to tactile switches, see [tactile vs linear switches](/blog/tactile-vs-linear-switches).
