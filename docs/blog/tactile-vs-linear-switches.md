# Tactile vs linear switches: what the bump changes

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Most mechanical keyboard advice ends at "pick linear, tactile or clicky". Clicky is easy to recognize. The harder choice is between tactile and linear, because neither makes a click and both can sound alike. This post covers what the difference actually is, what Cherry's spec sheets say, and recordings of both types so you can hear it rather than read about it.

## The difference in one line

A **linear** switch moves straight down with the same feel all the way. A **tactile** switch pushes back a little more at one point in the stroke, then gives way: that bump is where the key registers. Cherry describes its MX Brown bump as "focused and noticeable".

## On paper

From Cherry's MX2A product pages, checked on 28 November 2026:

| | MX Red | MX Black | MX Brown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | linear | linear | tactile |
| Operating force | 45 cN | 60 cN | 55 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.0 mm | 2.0 mm | 2.0 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm |

All three share the same travel. What changes is the spring and whether the force curve has a bump in it. The Brown page lists 55 cN in its highlights and 45 cN as actuation force in its properties; [Cherry MX Brown](/blog/cherry-mx-brown) goes into what the two figures mean.

## What you hear

Without a click mechanism, both types make most of their noise in the same two places: the stem hitting the bottom and the key springing back. So a tactile and a linear switch in the same keyboard can sound very close. What changes is rhythm: on a tactile switch the bump slightly changes how and when your finger bottoms out.

Keycaps, the case, foam and lubrication change the sound more than the switch type does, which is why the same switch sounds different in two keyboards. [Creamy keyboard sound](/blog/creamy-keyboard-sound) covers those parts.

## Which one to choose

| If you | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Want to feel each keystroke register | Tactile, such as MX Brown |
| Want a smooth, even stroke | Linear, such as MX Red |
| Rest your fingers heavily on the keys | A heavier linear such as [MX Black](/blog/cherry-mx-black), or a tactile |
| Want a click as well as a bump | [Clicky](/blog/clicky-switches), such as MX Blue |

The recordings give you the sound. For the feel there is no substitute for pressing the switches, for example with a switch tester.

## Hearing them on a Mac

KeyCream's settings window shows every pack as a card grouped into linear, tactile and clicky, and plays a few keys when you pick one. That makes it a quick way to hear the difference on the keyboard you already have. It changes what you hear, not how the keys feel.
