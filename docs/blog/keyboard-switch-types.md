# Keyboard switch types: linear, tactile, clicky

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Mechanical keyboard switches come in three basic types. The type decides how a key feels on the way down and a large part of how it sounds. This post explains each one with the numbers from Cherry's spec sheets and a recording from KeyCream's sound packs, so you can hear the difference instead of reading about it.

## The three types at a glance

| Type | Feel | Sound | Classic example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linear | smooth, straight down | bottom-out and return only | Cherry MX Red, MX Black |
| Tactile | a bump partway down | like linear, no click | Cherry MX Brown |
| Clicky | a bump and a click | a sharp click on every press | Cherry MX Blue |

## Linear

The stem slides straight down against the spring. Force rises evenly and nothing interrupts the stroke. Cherry's MX2A pages list the Red at 45 cN operating force and the Black at 60 cN, both with 2.0 mm pre-travel and 4.0 mm total travel.

## Tactile

A tactile switch has a bump in its stroke: resistance rises, then drops. You feel that the key has passed a point, without any extra sound from the switch. Cherry's MX2A Brown page lists it as tactile with no click, 2.0 mm pre-travel and 4.0 mm total travel, with 55 cN operating force in the highlights.

## Clicky

A clicky switch adds a separate mechanism that snaps and makes a click at the bump. That click is the point of the switch, and the reason it is the loudest type. Cherry lists the MX2A Blue as "tactile & clicky", with 2.2 mm pre-travel and 4.0 mm total travel.

## Beyond the three

Switch makers sell many variations:

- **Silent** linears and tactiles damp the bottom-out and the return.
- **Heavier and lighter springs** change the force without changing the type.
- **Magnetic** (Hall effect) switches sense position with a magnet instead of a metal contact. Many feel linear.

The type still tells you the most about what to expect.

## Mechanical keyboard switches and sound

The type sets the switch's part of the sound, but the keyboard around it matters as much: keycap material and thickness, the plate, the case, foam, and how hard you type. That is why two keyboards with the same switch can sound different. KeyCream's Cherry MX packs were recorded with PBT keycaps; the [sound pack page](/guide/sound-packs) lists how each pack was recorded.
