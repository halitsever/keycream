# Linear switches: what Cherry MX Red and Black sound like

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Linear switches are the simplest kind of mechanical keyboard switch: the key goes straight down and comes straight back up, with nothing in between. KeyCream has three linear recordings, Cherry MX Red, Cherry MX Black and NovelKeys Cream, and this post puts them next to each other with the numbers from the switch makers.

## What "linear" means

Every mechanical switch has a stem pushed up by a spring. In a linear switch, nothing else touches the stem on the way down. The resistance rises evenly with the spring until the key bottoms out. There is no bump to feel and no click to hear.

That differs from the other two families:

- **Tactile** switches add a bump partway down, such as Cherry MX Brown.
- **Clicky** switches add a bump and a click mechanism, such as Cherry MX Blue.

[Keyboard switch types](/blog/keyboard-switch-types) has all three side by side.

## Cherry MX Red and Black on paper

From Cherry's product pages for the current MX2A versions, checked on 28 November 2026:

| | Cherry MX Red | Cherry MX Black |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | linear | linear |
| Operating force | 45 cN | 60 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.0 mm | 2.0 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm |

Same travel, different springs. The Black takes a third more force to actuate, so it feels heavier. Neither has a bump or a click.

KeyCream's packs don't say which generation of each switch was recorded, so treat the table as Cherry's spec for the switch, not a measurement of the recording.

With a linear switch, almost all of the sound comes from the stem bottoming out and springing back, plus the keycap and the keyboard around it. That is why the same switch can sound quite different in two keyboards, and why recordings are easier to compare than descriptions.

## A note on magnetic switches

Magnetic (Hall effect) switches are a separate category that comes up in the same searches. They sense the key position with a magnet and a sensor instead of a metal contact, which lets some keyboards change the actuation point. Many of them have a linear feel. KeyCream has no recording of one, so there is nothing to play here.
