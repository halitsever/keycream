# What is thock? Thocky and clacky keyboards

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

"Thock" is the sound keyboard enthusiasts chase: deep, low and muffled, the opposite of a bright clack. It is a made-up word, not a measurement, so this post sticks to how people generally use it, what changes the sound of a keyboard, and recordings you can listen to instead of adjectives.

## The words people use

| Word | What people usually mean |
| --- | --- |
| Thock | Deep, low, muffled, a bit like a soft knock |
| Clack | Bright, sharp, higher pitched, with some ring |
| Creamy | Smooth and soft without scratch, often heard as somewhere between the two |
| Click | The distinct click of a clicky switch, a separate thing from all three |

These are informal and overlap. Two people can call the same keyboard thocky and clacky. That is a good reason to listen to recordings rather than trust labels, including the ones in this post.

## What makes a keyboard sound thocky

A switch without a click makes most of its sound when the stem hits the bottom and springs back. What you hear is that impact passed through the keycap, the plate and the case. So the parts around the switch shape the sound as much as the switch does. The ones people change most:

- **Keycaps.** Thicker caps and different plastics change the pitch. PBT caps are often described as lower-pitched than thin ABS ones.
- **Case and foam.** A hollow case resonates; foam between the plate and the case damps it.
- **Plate.** Its material and how it is mounted affect how much it rings.
- **Stabilizers.** Rattling stabilizers on the space bar and other long keys add clack.
- **Lubrication.** Lubed switches lose scratch and ping, which many people hear as a fuller sound.
- **The desk.** A desk mat absorbs some of what the case passes on.

Results depend on the whole keyboard, so change one thing at a time and listen. [Creamy keyboard sound](/blog/creamy-keyboard-sound) covers the same parts with the creamy sound in mind.

## Switch type and thock

Linear and tactile switches both have no click, so both can sound thocky; [tactile vs linear switches](/blog/tactile-vs-linear-switches) has recordings of each. Clicky switches add a high click in the middle of every press, which is the opposite of what people mean by thock. See [clicky switches](/blog/clicky-switches).

## Thock without a mechanical keyboard

If you type on a MacBook or a Magic Keyboard, the keys stay what they are. What you can change is what you hear: KeyCream plays a recording of a real switch for every key you press. Picking NovelKeys Cream, the pack it starts with, or Everglide Oreo gets you the deeper end of its seven packs. It changes the sound, not the feel. See [Using KeyCream](/guide/usage).
