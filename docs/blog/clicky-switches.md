# Clicky switches: where the click comes from

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Mechanical keyboard switches come in three types: linear, tactile and clicky. Clicky ones are the loud ones, and on purpose: they make a sharp click at the exact point the key registers. This post explains how that click is made, how to tell a clicky switch from a tactile one by ear, and gives you a recording to compare with.

## How a clicky switch clicks

On a linear or tactile switch, most of the sound comes from the key bottoming out and springing back. A clicky switch adds a third sound in the middle of the stroke, made by a part whose only job is to click.

Cherry describes how its MX Blue does it: the stem is made of two parts, and when the white part hits the blue stem after the tactile point, it triggers the click. You feel the bump and hear the click at the same moment, which tells you the key has registered before it reaches the bottom.

Other makers build clicky switches differently, but the idea is the same: a separate piece that snaps at the actuation point. The [Cherry MX Blue](/blog/cherry-mx-blue) post has Cherry's full specs for that switch.

## Clicky vs tactile vs linear

| | Linear | Tactile | Clicky |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bump you can feel | no | yes | yes |
| Click you can hear | no | no | yes |
| Cherry example | MX Red, MX Black | MX Brown | MX Blue, MX Green |
| KeyCream packs | NovelKeys Cream, MX Red, MX Black | MX Brown, Everglide Crystal Purple, Everglide Oreo | MX Blue |

Cherry's own switch list marks only two MX switches "with click": MX Blue and MX Green. It describes the Green as "loud, robust and noticeable". [Keyboard switch types](/blog/keyboard-switch-types) has one recording of each type.

## Telling them apart by ear

On a recording, a clicky switch gives you two sounds per press: the high click partway down and the lower sound of the key bottoming out. Press slowly and stop halfway and you still hear the click. A tactile switch stays quiet until the bottom. A linear switch sounds like the tactile one but without the change in rhythm the bump causes.

## Do blue switches always click?

Blue has become the usual colour for clicky switches because of the Cherry MX Blue, and many makers follow it. Colour is not a standard, though. Read the maker's description: "clicky" or "with click" is what you are looking for.

## Who picks clicky switches

People who like a clear signal for every keystroke, and people who simply like the sound. The cost is noise for everyone around you. If you want the click in your own ears only, an app can play it through headphones: KeyCream plays the MX Blue recording for every key you press on a Mac, and the [Cherry MX Blue post](/blog/cherry-mx-blue#mx-blue-sound-on-a-mac) shows the setting.
