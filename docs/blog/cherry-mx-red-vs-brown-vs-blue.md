# Cherry MX Red vs Brown vs Blue: hear the difference

<p class="post-meta">Halit Sever · 28 November 2026</p>

Red, Brown and Blue are the three Cherry MX switches most people compare first, because each one stands for a switch type: linear, tactile and clicky. Here are Cherry's numbers for each and a recording of each from KeyCream's sound packs. Play them one after another; it settles most of the question faster than any description.

## The specs

From Cherry's MX2A product pages, checked on 28 November 2026:

| | MX Red | MX Brown | MX Blue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | linear | tactile | tactile and clicky |
| Operating force (highlights) | 45 cN | 55 cN | 60 cN |
| Pre-travel | 2.0 mm | 2.0 mm | 2.2 mm |
| Total travel | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm | 4.0 mm |
| Click | no | no | yes |

The Brown and Blue pages also list a lower figure in their properties tables (45 cN and 50 cN). The packs don't say which generation of each switch was recorded, so these are Cherry's figures for the switches, not measurements of the recordings.

## Red vs Brown

Same travel, and neither clicks. The difference is the bump: the Brown has one, the Red does not, and the Red needs less force. Without a click, both make most of their sound at the bottom-out and the return.

## Brown vs Blue

Both have a bump. The Blue adds a click mechanism that snaps at the bump, so every press makes a sharp, high click. That click is the biggest difference you will hear in this comparison. The Blue also has slightly more pre-travel and needs more force.

## Red vs Blue

These are the two ends: smooth and quiet against bumpy and loud. If you share a room, this is usually the comparison that decides it.

## Which one fits

| If you want | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| A smooth stroke and no extra noise | Red |
| A bump you can feel, without a click | Brown |
| A bump and a click you can hear | Blue |
| A smooth stroke with a heavier spring | Black |

Personal taste decides the rest. KeyCream's three Everglide and NovelKeys packs are other points to compare: [linear switches](/blog/linear-keyboard-switches) covers NovelKeys Cream and MX Black, and [Cherry MX Brown](/blog/cherry-mx-brown) the two Everglide tactiles.
