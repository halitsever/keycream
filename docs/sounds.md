# Keyboard switch sounds

Pick a switch and type in the box. Every key plays a recording of a real mechanical switch, the same recordings the [KeyCream](/) app plays on a Mac. Sound starts with your first key press; each switch downloads once, about half a megabyte.

<SoundBoard />

## The seven switches

| Switch | Type | Recording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cherry MX Brown | tactile, no click | one take, PBT keycaps |
| Cherry MX Red | linear | one take, PBT keycaps |
| Cherry MX Black | linear, heavier | one take, PBT keycaps |
| Cherry MX Blue | tactile and clicky | one take, PBT keycaps |
| NovelKeys Cream | linear | one file per key |
| Everglide Crystal Purple | tactile | one take |
| Everglide Oreo | tactile | one take |

**Linear** switches go straight down, **tactile** ones have a bump you can feel, and **clicky** ones add an audible click. [Keyboard switch types](/blog/keyboard-switch-types) explains each with Cherry's specs.

## Brown switch sounds

Cherry MX Brown is selected when the page opens because it is the switch people most often ask about. It has a light tactile bump and no click, so unlike the clicky Blue it makes no click on each press. Switch between the three and type the same sentence to hear it. [Cherry MX Brown sound and feel](/blog/cherry-mx-brown) has Cherry's numbers, and [Red vs Brown vs Blue](/blog/cherry-mx-red-vs-brown-vs-blue) compares all three.

## How the page plays them

- Each switch's recording is cut into one short clip per key: the 26 letters, space, return, delete and shift. Other keys borrow a letter's clip.
- Every clip is leveled the way the app levels it, so one key does not jump out over the others.
- On a phone, where the on-screen keyboard often reports no key code, each typed character plays its letter instead.
- Nothing you type leaves your browser. The page only downloads the recordings.

## Get it in your Mac's menu bar

On a Mac, KeyCream plays the switch you choose for every key you press, in any app. It is free and open source, sits in the menu bar with no window, and only reads which key went down. See [Privacy](/guide/privacy).
